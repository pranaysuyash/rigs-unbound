# Tractor Restoration and Modular Growth

- Status: **Proposed exploration direction; not yet accepted or implemented**
- Date: 2026-07-25
- Trigger: project-owner proposal that the tractor begin dilapidated/basic, become visually robust through upgrades, and gain switchable additions
- Product phrase: **same bones, changing verbs**

## Recommendation

Yes: make the first tractor a rescue/restoration relationship rather than a disposable starter item.

The player should begin with a machine that is:

- visibly old, incomplete, and locally repaired;
- immediately drivable enough to be enjoyable;
- missing capabilities rather than merely suffering weak numbers;
- recognizable as the same character after every repair and transformation.

The robust tractor in the preferred visual direction should be an earned future state. It must not be a replacement model silently substituted after an upgrade animation.

## Three progression axes

Restoration, specialization, and switching should remain distinct.

| Axis               | Player question                       | What changes                                                                         | Guardrail                                                            |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Restoration        | “Can I bring this machine back?”      | reliability, missing systems, structural condition, damaged mounts, visible repairs  | avoid making the opening frustrating or using durability as busywork |
| Specialization     | “What kind of tractor am I building?” | engine tuning, traction, armor, power, cargo, sensors, module capacity and tradeoffs | no universal best build or one-way gear-score ladder                 |
| Switchable modules | “What verbs do I bring today?”        | front/rear/roof/cargo tools and their deployed states                                | modules remain physical, compatible, limited, and explainable        |

This separation prevents “repair the brakes” and “install an alien field generator” from becoming the same abstract upgrade button.

## Machine journey

| Phase          | Visual state                                                                         | Mechanical state                             | Emotional purpose            |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 0. Found       | flat tire or mismatched wheel, broken lamp, bent mount, exposed panel, smoke/sputter | drivable, but one or two verbs unavailable   | curiosity and responsibility |
| 1. Stabilized  | safe wheel, repaired steering/brakes, one working light, rough field repairs         | predictable handling and dependable restart  | trust                        |
| 2. Working     | restored front mount, first tool, storage, clearer mechanical rhythm                 | complete farming/traversal loop              | competence                   |
| 3. Specialized | chosen tires, suspension, power system, armor/cargo/sensor emphasis                  | meaningful build strengths and costs         | ownership                    |
| 4. Hybridized  | improbable but legible grafts using known hardpoints                                 | cross-mode or cross-environment capabilities | invention                    |
| 5. Storied     | repairs, trophies, stains, replacement panels and sound carry session history        | mastery rather than raw stat dominance       | attachment and identity      |

“Robust” means structurally convincing, capable, and visibly maintained. It does not have to mean clean, oversized, armored, or covered in every unlocked part.

## What should remain recognizable

The persistent identity layer should survive all legal builds:

- cab and roof silhouette;
- round work-light “face”;
- large rear / smaller front wheel stance unless a major locomotion conversion explicitly changes it;
- amber beacon or its visible mounting history;
- signature repaired panel/turquoise patch;
- stable chassis proportions and serial identity;
- accumulated meaningful scars and named repairs;
- core sound motifs.

The player should be able to recognize their tractor in silhouette or grayscale after removing UI, rarity colors, and nameplates.

## Four kinds of change

### 1. Core restoration

These are infrequent, workshop-scale repairs:

- steering, brakes, starter, cooling, wiring, fuel/energy system;
- wheel or suspension restoration;
- cab, frame, mount and power-bus repair;
- fixing a broken headlight or sensor.

Core restoration removes a specific limitation and changes audiovisual behavior. It should not create recurring maintenance chores unless those chores produce meaningful decisions.

### 2. Chassis tuning

These adjust feel and tradeoffs:

- torque versus top speed;
- soft field suspension versus stiff road response;
- soil traction versus road efficiency;
- cooling versus armor;
- cargo capacity versus turning and acceleration;
- battery reserve versus burst output.

Tuning must be felt in steering, traction, load, sound, animation, route choice, or recovery—not only in a stat card.

### 3. Swappable physical modules

Proposed initial hardpoints:

- **front:** plow, loader, ram/brush guard, cutter, snow/soil blade;
- **rear:** seeder/spreader, trailer hitch, winch, harvester, deployable barrier;
- **roof/utility:** work lights, sensor mast, defense/repellent emitter, weather equipment;
- **cargo/power:** water tank, battery, seed/ammunition bin, repair kit, fuel reserve.

Start with two meaningful hardpoints in the first playable. More sockets should be earned only when the UI, silhouette, physics and decision load remain readable.

### 4. Deployed states

Switching a module and changing an installed module’s state are different:

- plow raised / lowered / angled;
- lights narrow / wide / pulsed;
- trailer hauling / detached as barricade;
- seeder farming / defensive spread pattern;
- winch attached / pulling / released;
- sprayer water / coolant / approved repellent payload.

Deployed-state changes can happen during play because the physical module is already installed. Full module swaps normally happen at a workshop, garage, carrier, or other believable service point.

## Switching rules

### Default

- Large modules switch at the workshop.
- Presets remember compatible loadouts but do not bypass inventory, condition, mass, power, clearance or hardpoint rules.
- Switching has a visible physical sequence and a clear before/after capability explanation.
- Removed modules remain owned objects with condition and history; they do not disappear into an untraceable stat.
- A failed or incompatible swap explains the exact conflict.

### Later field switching

Field swapping can itself be an earned capability:

- tool trailer or mobile service rig;
- crane/drone assistant;
- compact quick-coupler for a limited module class;
- temporary emergency attachment;
- co-op support vehicle.

This makes convenience part of progression without allowing magical hot-swapping from an infinite inventory.

## Modules should create multiple verbs and real costs

| Module             | Day/work value                        | Night/crisis value                              | Cost or limitation                              |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Wide plow          | open soil, clear debris, shape routes | push threats, expose burrows, reinforce lanes   | mass, turning radius, front clearance           |
| Seeder/spreader    | sow crops or treatments               | lay slowing/repellent zones                     | consumes cargo, rear hardpoint occupied         |
| Work-light rig     | extend work time, inspect machinery   | reveal or repel light-sensitive threats         | power draw, visibility to some enemies          |
| Trailer            | haul crops, salvage or tools          | detach as barrier, rescue cargo/NPCs            | acceleration, reversing difficulty, route width |
| Winch              | tow machines, logs and wrecks         | rescue, pull barricades or dislodge threats     | rear mount, setup time, anchor requirement      |
| Water/sprayer tank | water crops, wash/prepare surfaces    | cool systems, extinguish fire, disperse hazards | changing mass, refill dependency                |
| Armor cage         | protect cab and modules               | survive direct pressure                         | heat, mass, poorer visibility/agility           |
| Boost unit         | climb, cross gaps, time trial         | escape or emergency reposition                  | fuel/heat, fragility, reduced cargo/power       |
| Sensor mast        | find salvage, soil and buried signal  | reveal lanes, weak points or incoming waves     | vulnerable, power draw, high profile            |

An upgrade is interesting when it creates a new approach and a new problem.

## Progression and economy

Avoid a single gear score. Progress can use the existing grammar:

- **Scrap:** restore or fabricate known parts;
- **Parts:** concrete compatible objects with provenance and condition;
- **Insight:** understand strange signals, systems, recipes and module categories;
- **Favor:** gain workshop help, blueprints, services, trust and loaners;
- **Mastery/history:** unlock tuning options through demonstrated use, not spending.

Important modules should come from different stories:

- salvage and restoration;
- helping a character or settlement;
- finding a blueprint;
- winning or solving a vehicle-specific challenge;
- trading a real part;
- combining known systems after experimentation.

Buying everything from one shop would weaken the world and the tractor relationship.

## First-playable version

Keep the full system out of the first loop. Test the emotional and mechanical premise with:

### Opening tractor

- drivable immediately;
- one unreliable/sputtering audiovisual state that does not randomly steal control;
- one broken work light;
- visibly bent or incomplete front mount;
- mismatched repaired panels and one memorable inherited patch;
- limited rear power or cargo connection.

### Early restoration

Within the first few minutes:

1. stabilize the tractor;
2. repair the front mount;
3. install or restore the broad plow;
4. feel a clear change in handling, sound, silhouette and available interaction.

### First real choice

Before or at the first night, choose one support direction:

- work-light rig: information and power tradeoff;
- seeder/spreader: farming efficiency and defensive zone tradeoff;
- trailer/hitch: hauling and barricade tradeoff.

The plow supplies the shared baseline verb. The support module supplies the first player-authored identity.

### Dawn consequence

At dawn:

- the same tractor carries visible damage and use marks;
- the selected module explains part of what survived;
- one repair or tuning choice becomes available;
- the player can see—not merely read—that the machine is becoming theirs.

## Data and state implications

The existing `VehicleBlueprint` / `VehicleInstance` boundary remains correct.

`VehicleBlueprint` should eventually define:

- hardpoint types and transforms;
- chassis envelopes and mass/power/clearance budgets;
- compatible module categories and explicit incompatibilities;
- core identity asset keys;
- permitted locomotion conversions.

`VehicleInstance` should eventually own:

- restoration state and condition by meaningful component;
- installed component-instance references;
- saved loadout presets;
- module condition, repairs and provenance;
- visible patch/scar/history records;
- mastery and named outcomes.

Derived capabilities such as `can_plow`, `can_tow`, or `can_reveal_hidden_threats` come from the installed valid composition. A UI, renderer or client cannot grant them directly.

## UI and workshop implications

The workshop should show the actual machine:

- orbit/inspect the tractor rather than replacing it with detached cards;
- highlight physical hardpoints and clearance envelopes;
- compare gained verb, lost verb, mass, power, handling and environmental access;
- preview silhouette and deployed states;
- explain why a part is incompatible;
- allow reversible experimentation before confirmation;
- preserve favorite presets without implying modules are duplicated;
- show repair history and provenance alongside performance.

The interface should answer:

1. What will this let me do?
2. What will it stop or worsen?
3. Where will it fit?
4. What does it cost to run or carry?
5. Is this the same part that has history with me?

## Failure modes to avoid

- starting so broken that movement is annoying;
- upgrades that only add percentages;
- every new tier being universally better;
- robust becoming visually over-armored or over-greebled;
- arbitrary field hot-swapping with no physical explanation;
- a socket explosion that overwhelms controller/touch UI;
- hiding incompatibility behind a generic “requirements not met” message;
- repair becoming a tax or waiting timer;
- replacing the original tractor mesh and losing its history;
- selling power before the player understands the base machine;
- modules that look different but preserve identical handling and verbs;
- damage decals that randomly rewrite established repair history.

## Validation questions

- Do players call it “my tractor” after one loop?
- Can they identify what changed without opening the stats screen?
- Does the first module choice alter both day and night decisions?
- Can players explain the cost of their selected module?
- Does the dilapidated opening create affection rather than irritation?
- Does the robust state still read as the original machine?
- Can a module be removed without corrupting history or capabilities?
- Are switch rules understandable on keyboard, gamepad and touch?
- Does the silhouette remain readable at tactical and mobile camera sizes?

## Decision effect

- The first-playable tractor should start damaged/basic and become visibly more complete.
- The robust preferred concept becomes an aspirational state, not the starting state.
- Restoration, specialization, module swapping and deployed states remain separate concepts.
- The first slice tests one restored signature tool plus one support-module choice.
- Large module switching is workshop-bound by default; field switching is a later earned capability.
- Status remains **Proposed** until a paper flow, visual evolution board and instrumented playable validate it.

## Anything else?

The best final tractor should not look as if every upgrade was installed. It should look as if the player made a sequence of decisions, solved particular problems, and kept a few beloved scars.
